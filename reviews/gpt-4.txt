# 🚨 Don’t overcorrect: “artifact detected” is true, but the conclusion needs precision

You wrote:

> “The crossover may not exist with proper unfolding”

Careful: there is no single “proper unfolding.” There are *good unfoldings* and *bad unfoldings*, and different unfoldings can legitimately change long-range statistics if they remove slightly different low-frequency trends.

Long-range rigidity metrics (Σ², Δ₃) are **notoriously sensitive** to unfolding at larger L because:

* they integrate fluctuations across windows,
* any small systematic bias in the local density estimate propagates.

So what you discovered might be:

1. a pure artifact (100% unfolding noise), **or**
2. a real effect that’s being masked or shifted by some unfoldings, **or**
3. a real effect plus a big unfolding bias (most likely).

You can’t declare it dead yet. But you can (correctly) declare your *previous universality claim* dead.

## 3) Add sanity checks on the unfolding itself

Every unfolding should pass these tests:

### Density test

After unfolding:

* mean spacing should be ~1
* the local density should be flat (no drift)
* the empirical counting function should match x(t)

### Residual trend test

Compute:
[
\delta_n = x_n - n
]
This should behave like a stationary fluctuation, not a trending curve.

If (\delta_n) has visible drift, your unfolding is wrong.

This is trivial to implement and catches half of these issues immediately.
